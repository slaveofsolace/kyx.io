# Strict self-review: Press Hall visual strike v2

Decision: **NOT_APPROVED for G5. Retain only as a bounded, non-default evidence/source candidate.**

## What this strike genuinely improved

- Eight unsupported v1 hanging sheets/spines are gone. Four replacement print-stock assemblies now have rollers, end housings, suspension cables, and explicit support metadata.
- Paper Drop now reads as a mechanical transition: an oiled folded-stock chute is attached to a dark load frame, braced into a banded intake roller, and reinforced by a visible crease, clamps, suspension, and three downward registration markers.
- The north/south press assemblies gained crossheads, tie rods, collars, diagonal braces, drive motors, cooling fins, flywheels, and logical conduit routes.
- Weathered procedural ceramic, fibrous stock, oxidized graphite, ink grime, worn vermilion/amber, sparse floor transfer, wall-base grime, and oxide drips reduce the sterile v1 finish.
- The frozen gameplay envelopes remain intact: all ten replacement groups are contained, authority-collision objects are absent, and revision 1 remains the catalog default.
- Independent verification passed 18/18 structural and artifact-integrity checks. Detail counts are 42 foundry-machinery, 32 fastener, 29 support/clamp, 26 conduit/cable, 26 Paper Drop identity, 21 surface-history, 12 safety-signage, and four supported-stock objects.

## Why this is self-rejected

- At gameplay distance, the dominant room language is still a repeated kit cadence of rectangular floor plates, slab covers, cylindrical reactor shells, straight beams, and suspended panels. Added detail dresses that cadence but does not yet replace it with a convincing final industrial silhouette.
- The new machinery is strongest in the close and Paper Drop views. In the gameplay/far views it is secondary to inherited blockout-scale forms, so the room does not yet achieve a bespoke foundry identity at combat distance.
- Paper Drop is now mechanically legible and visibly supported, but its three planar guide leaves and broad folded chute remain visually simple. It needs a more resolved feed path, side housing, catch/landing relationship, and stronger integration with the wall opening before acceptance.
- Surface history is procedural and deliberately sparse. It lacks authored UV/decal storytelling, localized leaks, edge abrasion, fastener staining, maintenance patches, and material transitions at shipping density.
- The v2 review boards are deterministic evidence sheets, not polished presentation boards; they have no labels and some crops still emphasize inherited forms.
- Static Blender renders cannot establish gameplay readability, combat occlusion, team-color separation, color-vision behavior, or the shipping post stack.
- No runtime triangle/draw/material/shader/memory/frame-time budget, in-engine no-snag pass, human visual review, or representative combat playtest has been completed.

## Required next art pass

1. Redesign the large press/reactor/cover silhouettes so the room no longer reads as dressed graybox modules while keeping the ten envelopes frozen.
2. Resolve Paper Drop as one continuous feed machine from upper stock intake through the wall opening to a believable landing/catch condition.
3. Add authored mid-frequency construction detail and localized material storytelling that remains visible at gameplay distance.
4. Produce labeled review boards with matched camera positions and explicit v1/v2 comparisons.
5. Only after a strict visual candidate clears human review, stage it behind an explicit non-default runtime preview and measure performance/no-snag behavior.
